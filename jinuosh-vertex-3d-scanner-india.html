What does a Vertex scanner do?

  • Three scan modes in one scanner: high-speed scanning with crossed blue laser lines, precision scanning with 7 blue laser lines, and deep-hole scanning with 1 single blue laser line. You switch modes on the scanner.
  • Blue laser: scans shiny and polished steel surfaces as well as dark and matte parts.
  • Class II laser: eye safe.
  • Resolution and volume accuracy: maximum resolution 0.01 mm; volume accuracy 0.015 mm + 0.035 mm/m (0.015 mm + 0.025 mm/m with photogrammetry).
  • Output: STL, PLY, ASC, TXT and other formats that open in your CAD software.
  • Portable: the Vertex3D handheld unit is about 340 x 140 x 75 mm and weighs about 1.07 kg (Vertex3D Pro: about 1.15 kg), so it works in tight spaces and on large objects. Dimensions per the JINUOSH sheet (approximate).

How accurate is the Vertex3D scanner?

The JINUOSH specification gives a highest precision of up to 0.020 mm for Vertex3D and Vertex3D Pro, and a volume accuracy of 0.015 mm + 0.035 mm/m. Accuracy in a real job also depends on the part, its surface and the setup.

What is the difference between Vertex3D and Vertex3D Pro?

Both reach 0.020 mm precision and a 650 x 550 mm scanning field. Vertex3D has 26 crossed blue laser lines and scans up to 5,400,000 measurements per second. Vertex3D Pro has 50 crossed lines and scans up to 7,100,000 measurements per second.

Can it scan shiny metal and deep holes?

The blue laser scans shiny and polished steel. A separate single-line mode is for deep holes. For the maximum hole depth, ask us, as we do not publish a figure.
