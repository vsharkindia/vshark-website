Eight questions to ask every 3D scanner maker (use the same list for each)

  1. What is the stated accuracy, and is it for the device or for a volume? Ask for the test method.
  2. What is the maximum scanning field and the working distance?
  3. How fast does it scan, and in which modes (large area, fine detail, deep holes)?
  4. How does it handle shiny or dark surfaces? (Vertex3D and Vertex3D Pro blue lasers can scan shiny and polished steel; ask others the same.)
  5. Is the software licence perpetual or yearly, and what does an upgrade cost?
  6. What PC is required? (Vertex: Windows 10 or later 64-bit, NVIDIA GTX/RTX class card with 8 GB VRAM, Intel Core i7-13700H or better, 32 GB RAM.)
  7. Who installs, trains and supports you in your city, and what does the warranty cover?
  8. What is the total cost: scanner, software, markers or accessories, PC, training, support?

Read each maker's figures on its own datasheet or website, and check the date of the document.

How to test a scanner on your own part

  1. Pick one real part with a known tolerance (a gauge-checked reference is ideal).
  2. Ask each supplier to scan that same part under the same conditions.
  3. Compare the deviation colour map and the dimensions against your reference, and time the whole job.
  4. Ask for the file formats you need (STL, STEP via your CAD software).
