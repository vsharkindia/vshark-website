How to 3D Scan Shiny, Reflective and Dark Parts

How to 3D scan shiny, reflective and dark parts: when spray helps, markers, scan angle, scan modes, and what the Vertex3D does and does not solve.

You can 3D scan shiny, reflective and dark parts by controlling three things: the surface, the geometry and the scanner settings. A light, removable matte spray fixes the surface only when it is needed. Reflective markers on and around the part, and scan angles that avoid direct glare, fix the geometry. Scanner settings, where the software offers them, help with the rest. Our JINUOSH Vertex3D and Vertex3D Pro scan shiny and polished steel, so spray is not always needed. Why are shiny and dark parts hard to scan?

A scanner works by reading the laser light that comes back from the part. Three surface types disturb that signal:

  • Shiny and mirror-like surfaces (polished steel, chrome, machined faces) throw the light away like a mirror, so the camera sees nothing at one angle and a glare spot at another.
  • Dark and black surfaces absorb light, so the return is weak and the scan comes out thin or noisy.
  • Transparent and translucent surfaces (glass, clear plastic) let the light pass through or scatter inside, so the surface position is wrong or missing.

The result on screen is holes, noise, spikes or a surface that looks rough when the part is smooth. The fixes below are ordered from the lightest to the heaviest.

Step 1: Try the scanner first, before any spray

Start without spray. Our Vertex3D and Vertex3D Pro scan shiny and polished steel directly, so shiny and polished steel on these two scanners needs no spray. Run a short test scan on the shiniest face and look at the live view for holes or noise. If the face comes in cleanly, carry on.

Step 2: Change the angle before you change the part

Most glare problems are about the angle between the laser, the camera and the surface.

  • Tilt the scanner slightly so the mirror reflection does not point straight back at the camera.
  • Avoid scanning a flat, polished face square-on from directly above.
  • Scan curved and flat shiny faces from two or three slightly different directions and let the software merge the passes.
  • Keep the scanner at its recommended working distance. The maker lists a 300 mm reference distance for the Vertex3D and Vertex3D Pro (200 mm in fine mode), so hold the distance the live view shows as good rather than guessing.

Step 3: Check the scanner settings and scan mode

If the scanning software offers an exposure or brightness setting, try a lower value on bright faces and a higher one on dark faces; check the live view. The Vertex scanners also have separate scan modes: crossed blue laser lines for high-speed scanning, 7 blue laser lines for precision scanning and a single line for deep-hole scanning (maker's brochure; the Vertex3D uses 26 crossed lines and the Vertex3D Pro 50). Change to the mode that suits the feature rather than forcing one mode across the whole part.

Step 4: Use reflective markers on the surroundings

Reflective markers do not have to sit on the shiny face. A good layout is:

  • Place markers on the part where the surface is dull, and on the fixture, table or a neutral frame around it where the part itself is too shiny to stick to.
  • Spread them irregularly, not in straight lines, and keep several in view at all times.
  • Never move markers once the scan has started.

The Vertex3D kit includes 6 mm and 3 mm reflective markers. With markers, the scanner can align each pass even where the shiny surface itself gives little to lock on to.

Step 5: Use a light, removable matte spray only when needed

When a surface still gives holes (a chrome face, glass, a very dark glossy plastic), a thin matte spray turns it into a surface the scanner can read.

  • Use a very thin, even coat. A thicker coat adds a layer to the surface, so keep it as thin as the surface allows.
  • Spray only the problem faces, not the whole part.
  • Use a removable spray and clean the part afterwards.
  • Check that the part may be sprayed at all. Some parts, such as museum pieces, must not be touched, and for these the angle, marker and settings steps matter most.
  • After scanning a sprayed part, check a known feature against a gauge or caliper to confirm the size is still right.

FAQ

Common questions.

Can you 3D scan shiny metal?

Yes, for shiny and polished steel. Our JINUOSH Vertex3D and Vertex3D Pro blue-laser scanners scan it directly. Other shiny metals such as chrome or aluminium can be harder, so we run a test scan on the shiniest face first and use markers, angle changes or a light spray only where needed.

Do I always need scanning spray?

No. Spray is a last step for faces that still give holes, such as chrome, glass or very dark glossy plastic. Shiny and polished steel on the Vertex3D and Vertex3D Pro scans without it.

Does scanning spray change the dimensions?

A thin coat adds a very small layer to the surface. We keep the coat as thin as possible and check known features against a gauge or caliper after scanning a sprayed part.

How do I scan a black part?

Black surfaces absorb light. If the software offers an exposure setting, try a higher value, change the angle and spray only the worst faces.

Can you scan glass or transparent plastic?

Not directly, because the laser passes through or scatters. A thin removable spray is normally needed, or the part can be measured another way.

What are reflective markers for?

They give the scanner fixed points to align each pass. On shiny parts they can be placed on the surroundings or fixture, where the part itself gives little to lock on to.

Can you scan a part that cannot be sprayed?

Often yes, using markers, angle and scanner settings, but it takes more care and some small gaps may remain. We tell you before we start.
