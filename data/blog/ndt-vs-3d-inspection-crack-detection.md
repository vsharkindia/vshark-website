Many people search "crack detection" when a part has failed, a casting looks suspect or a machine part has worn. The words sound alike, but dimensional inspection and NDT answer two different questions. This guide gives the honest split, so you send the part to the right place the first time.

### What is the difference between 3D inspection and NDT?
**3D inspection (3D scanning and CMM)** asks: is the part the right shape and size? A scanner or CMM arm captures the surface geometry and the software compares it with the CAD model or drawing. The result is a deviation colour map, sections and a report of dimensions.

**NDT** asks: is the material sound? NDT methods look for flaws in the material itself, such as cracks, porosity, voids and inclusions, without cutting the part. They detect flaws whether or not the flaw changes the outside shape.

### What can 3D inspection find?
- Wear and erosion: where a surface has worn away compared with the original shape
- Warpage and distortion in castings, moulded parts, sheet metal and welded fabrications
- Dents, bends, missing or excess material
- Dimensions and hole positions that are out of tolerance
- Gaps and fit problems between mating parts
- Change over time: a scan before and after use, to see how much a part has moved or worn

### What can it NOT find?
- Cracks inside the part, and porosity or voids under the surface
- Hairline surface cracks, which are usually too fine to show in scan data
- Changes in material strength, hardness or heat-treatment problems
- Weld quality below the surface

If a crack is wide enough to open a visible gap, or has caused the part to bend or sag, the scan can show the resulting deformation. That is a shape change, not a crack test, and it should not replace NDT where safety or certification matters.

### Which NDT method suits which problem?
A short guide only. An NDT lab chooses the method for your part and material.
- **Dye penetrant:** surface-breaking cracks on most non-porous materials.
- **Magnetic particle:** surface and near-surface cracks in magnetic steels and irons.
- **Ultrasonic:** flaws inside thicker sections, and wall thickness.
- **X-ray or industrial CT:** internal porosity, voids and cracks in castings and complex parts.

### When should you use both?
A common case is a cast or machined part that must be both sound and in tolerance. The NDT lab confirms there are no cracks or voids. We then scan or measure the part to confirm it matches the drawing, or to find out why it does not fit. Another common case is a worn part that has to be rebuilt: NDT checks the old part for cracks, and scan-to-CAD captures the shape for the new one.

### FAQ

**Do you do NDT or crack detection?**
No. We do 3D scan inspection and CMM arm inspection, which check shape and size. For cracks you need an NDT method such as dye penetrant, magnetic particle, ultrasonic or X-ray/CT testing from an NDT lab.

**Can a 3D scanner detect cracks?**
Only in a limited way. A scan shows shape, so it can show a crack that has opened a visible gap or deformed the part. It does not reliably show hairline surface cracks and cannot see cracks inside the material.

**What is the difference between NDT and dimensional inspection?**
Dimensional inspection (3D scanning and CMM) checks whether a part is the right shape and size. NDT checks whether the material is sound, without damaging the part.

**What can 3D inspection find in a worn or damaged part?**
Wear, warpage, dents, bends, missing or excess material, out-of-tolerance dimensions and fit problems. It shows them against CAD or a drawing as a colour map and a report.

**Can you tell me which test my part needs?**
We can tell you whether your question is about shape and size, which we measure, or about material flaws, which need NDT. We do not choose or certify the NDT method; an NDT lab does that.

**Should I scan a part before or after NDT?**
Usually after the NDT confirms the part is sound, and before you spend money rebuilding or machining it. If the part has failed, NDT first helps to find why; scanning then records the shape for repair or replacement.
