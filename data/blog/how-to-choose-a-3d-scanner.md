Choose a 3D scanner by four things: the size of your parts, the tolerance you must prove, the surfaces you scan (shiny, dark, deep holes) and how many jobs you do in a year. Handheld laser scanners suit most workshop parts, tracker-based systems suit large or long parts, and structured-light scanners suit small, detailed parts. If you scan only a few parts a year, hiring a scanning service usually makes more sense than buying. ### Which type of 3D scanner fits which job?
| Type | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Handheld laser (for example our Vertex3D and Vertex3D Pro) | Castings, mouldings, sheet metal, vehicle panels, parts with no drawing, scanning at your site or ours | Large parts need markers or photogrammetry to control error |
| Tracker-based (for example our Vertex Tracker) | Large or long parts where error must not build up, wider scanning field | Bigger system and a larger investment; more setup than a handheld |
| Structured light (fixed or tripod units) | Small, detailed parts that fit one view, such as small components and dental or jewellery-size items | Slower for large parts; less flexible on site |
| Scanning service | Occasional jobs, one-off reverse engineering, proof reports, no staff to run a scanner | You depend on a provider's schedule |

### What should I check before I choose?
- **Part size.** Measure your largest and smallest parts. The Vertex3D and Vertex3D Pro list a maximum scanning field of 650 x 550 mm and the Vertex Tracker 1100 x 800 mm (JINUOSH brochure, as of November 2026). The maker also lists a recommended sample size of more than 10 mm for the Vertex3D and Vertex3D Pro.
- **Tolerance.** Write down the tightest tolerance you must prove. The maker states up to 0.020 mm for the Vertex3D and Vertex3D Pro and up to 0.025 mm for the Vertex Tracker. These are the maker's stated best-case values, not a guarantee on your part. The full meaning is in [handheld 3D scanner accuracy](/blog/handheld-3d-scanner-accuracy?utm_source=website&utm_medium=seo&utm_campaign=2026-11-s39&utm_content=blog-how-to-choose). For the tightest tolerances, a CMM may be the better tool for those features.
- **Surface.** Shiny, dark or transparent surfaces make scanning harder. Our Vertex3D and Vertex3D Pro scan shiny and polished steel. Ask any seller to show your kind of surface.
- **Deep holes and details.** The Vertex models list a single-line deep-hole scanning mode and a 7-line precision mode, switchable on the scanner (JINUOSH brochure). Ask for a test on your part to see how deep a hole it reaches.
- **Where you scan.** On the shop floor, in a customer's plant or at a site visit. A handheld is portable (the Vertex3D weighs 1.07 kg and the Vertex3D Pro 1.15 kg).
- **Your computer.** The maker lists a minimum PC: Windows 10 or later 64-bit, an NVIDIA GPU such as an RTX 4060 or better with 8 GB VRAM, an Intel Core i7-13700H or better and 32 GB RAM.
- **Output.** Scanners give STL, PLY, ASC and TXT. To get an editable CAD model you also need scan-to-CAD work in your CAD software. See [what files you get from a 3D scan](/blog/3d-scan-file-formats?utm_source=website&utm_medium=seo&utm_campaign=2026-11-s39&utm_content=blog-how-to-choose).
- **Who will run it.** A scanner needs a trained operator and a scan plan. Training at delivery and installation at your site are part of every scanner we supply in India and the UAE.

### Should I buy a scanner or hire a scanning service?
Buy when you scan often, want parts scanned in house and have a person to run it. Hire a service when you have occasional jobs, a one-off reverse engineering task or need a proof report without owning equipment. A simple test: if you cannot name at least a regular stream of parts to scan each month, start with a service and buy later. The scans you commission first show you what a scanner must handle.

### Questions to ask any seller before you pay
1. Can you scan my part, at my size and tolerance, and show the result?
2. What is the stated accuracy and how is it measured? Is that for a small part or a large one?
3. What does the price include: software licence, training, installation, warranty?
4. What computer do I need?
5. Who supports me after delivery, and where?
Ask for answers in writing. A good seller answers with a setup, not a slogan.

### How do I decide, step by step?
1. List your parts: largest size, smallest feature, material, surface.
2. Write the tightest tolerance and what you must prove to the customer.
3. Count jobs per month for the next year.
4. If fewer than a steady stream, hire a service first. If more, shortlist handheld or tracker by part size.
5. Ask for a test on your kind of part before deciding.

### FAQ

**How do I choose a 3D scanner for my business?**
Match it to your largest part, your tightest tolerance, your surfaces and your monthly workload. Handheld laser suits most workshop parts, tracker systems suit large parts, structured light suits small detailed parts.

**Which is better, a handheld laser or a structured-light 3D scanner?**
Neither is better for everything. Handheld laser scanners are flexible and portable and suit larger or awkward parts. Structured-light units suit small parts that fit in one view. Choose by your part size and where you scan.

**When do I need a tracker-based 3D scanner?**
When parts are large or long and error must not build up across the scan. The Vertex Tracker lists a scanning field of 1100 x 800 mm and a stated precision of up to 0.025 mm (maker's value).

**Should I buy a 3D scanner or hire a scanning service?**
If you scan only occasionally, hire a service first. Buy when you have a steady stream of parts, a trained operator and a reason to scan in house.

**How accurate are the Vertex scanners?**
